FAQ

common questions.

Is MoneyGator free? ↓
Yes — the core experience is free. You get 3 lessons per week, one connected account, and basic goal tracking. A Premium plan at $9/month unlocks unlimited daily lessons, all accounts, and subscription detection. Early waitlist users get extended free access.
How does MoneyGator access my bank data? ↓
We connect to your bank account using bank-level secure technology, the same approach used by major financial apps. MoneyGator has read-only access — we see your transactions and balances. We never move, store passwords, or touch your money.
What makes MoneyGator different from Credit Karma or Rocket Money? ↓
Those apps show you your data and stop there. MoneyGator uses your data as the starting point for education. When we detect a subscription you forgot, you get a lesson on why subscriptions are designed to be invisible — and exactly what to do about it today. We don't just report. We teach.
When is the iOS app launching? ↓
We are in active development. The app is built and running on a physical iPhone — bank connection is our final milestone before public launch. Join the waitlist to be first in line and get free access for early users.
Is my financial data safe? ↓
Yes. We use bank-level 256-bit encryption. Your login credentials go directly to your bank — MoneyGator never sees your username or password. We do not sell your data to third parties, ever.
I have no idea about finance. Is MoneyGator for me? ↓
Especially for you. MoneyGator is built for people who feel anxious about money but don't know where to start. Every lesson is designed to be understood in 60 seconds, without any prior financial knowledge. No jargon. No math. Just one clear thing to do today.

Financial Literacy

Why America Has a Financial Literacy Crisis — And What It's Costing Us

1 in 3 Americans can't cover a $400 emergency. 44% carry credit card debt month to month. These aren't personal failures — they're symptoms of a system that never taught people how money actually works.

Financial literacy in the United States is at a critical low. Only 34% of Americans can correctly answer basic questions about interest rates, inflation, and risk diversification. That number has been declining for over a decade.

The consequences are real. The average American household carries $6,500 in credit card debt. Nearly half of workers say financial stress affects their productivity at work.

Why this is getting worse, not better

Financial products have gotten more complex while financial education has stayed the same. Subscription services are designed to be forgotten. Credit card rewards programs are engineered to maximize spending.

What actually works

Research consistently shows that financial education works best when it is specific, timely, and actionable. A lesson that arrives the day before your credit card payment is due — and tells you exactly what to do — changes behavior. That's what MoneyGator is built to do.

Personal Finance

The Real Benefit of Tracking Your Finances — It's Not What You Think

Most people track their spending hoping to feel more in control. What actually changes behavior is the gap between what you think you spend and what you actually spend.

Budgeting apps have been around for decades. Yet the average American's financial health hasn't improved. If tracking worked the way we think it does, we'd have seen results by now.

The awareness gap is the real problem

Studies show that people underestimate their discretionary spending by 40% on average. This isn't dishonesty — it's just how memory works. We remember the big purchases and forget the small ones.

Tracking without understanding is just noise

A pie chart showing you spent $400 on food last month doesn't tell you what to change. The most valuable financial habit isn't tracking. It's understanding. That's what MoneyGator builds.
